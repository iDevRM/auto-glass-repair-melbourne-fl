What Is Auto Glass Repair in Melbourne, FL?

Auto glass repair covers two different jobs that often get lumped together. The first is chip and crack repair, where a technician injects a clear resin into a small break in the windshield to stop it from spreading and restore most of its strength. The second is full replacement, where the entire windshield or window is removed and swapped for a new piece of safety glass, bonded in with automotive-grade urethane adhesive.

Which one you need depends mostly on size and location. A chip the size of a coin, sitting away from the edge of the glass, is almost always a repair job. A long crack, damage near the frame, or anything sitting directly in your line of sight usually means replacement is the safer call. Brevard County drivers deal with this more than most, thanks to a mix of highway gravel on I-95 and US-1, sudden afternoon storms, and the kind of year-round sun exposure that makes existing chips spread faster than they would up north.

Melbourne Auto Glass Repair handles both jobs on the same visit whenever possible, using OEM-equivalent glass and urethane adhesives rated for Florida heat and humidity, so the bond cures properly even on a 95-degree July afternoon.

When Do You Need Auto Glass Repair?

Most drivers wait too long to deal with a chip because it does not look urgent yet. These are the situations where calling sooner saves you money and hassle later:

  • A fresh rock chip from the Beeline or I-95. Melbourne’s commuter corridors kick up plenty of loose gravel, and a same-day repair before dirt and moisture get into the chip gives resin the best chance to bond.
  • A crack that grew overnight. Temperature swings between a hot afternoon and an air-conditioned garage cause glass to expand and contract, which is a common reason a small chip turns into a foot-long crack by morning.
  • Damage sitting in your direct line of sight. Even a small chip there is a distraction and, depending on placement, can fail a vehicle inspection.
  • A side or rear window that no longer seals properly. A window that will not go up all the way, or one that rattles in its track, points to a broken regulator or damaged glass that needs attention before the next rainstorm.
  • You just had a windshield replaced somewhere else and the lane-keeping warning light came on. That almost always means the camera behind the mirror needs to be recalibrated.

Florida’s Zero-Deductible Windshield Law

Florida is one of the few states where comprehensive auto insurance is required by law to cover windshield damage with no deductible. Under Florida Statute 627.7288, if your policy includes comprehensive coverage, your insurer cannot charge you a deductible for a windshield repair or replacement, even if your regular comprehensive deductible is several hundred dollars for other kinds of damage.

In practice, this means most Melbourne drivers carrying comprehensive coverage pay nothing out of pocket for a covered windshield claim. We handle the insurance paperwork directly with your carrier whenever you want us to, so you are not stuck on hold trying to figure out a claim number while your windshield sits cracked in the driveway. If you are not sure whether your policy includes comprehensive coverage, your declarations page will list it, or we can help you check before we schedule the visit.

Side and rear window damage, and ADAS calibration tied to a covered windshield replacement, are typically included under the same claim, which is worth mentioning to your insurer up front rather than after the fact.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Florida still replace windshields for free?

In most cases, yes. Florida law requires comprehensive auto insurance policies to cover full glass replacement with no deductible, so if you carry comprehensive coverage, your windshield replacement is typically handled at no cost to you. Chip repairs are usually covered the same way, since insurers would rather pay for a small repair than a full replacement later.

Can you legally drive with a cracked windshield in Florida?

Florida law allows minor chips outside the driver’s direct line of sight, but a crack that spreads across the glass, blocks your view, or affects the edge of the windshield can get you pulled over and can also make the glass structurally unsafe in a crash. If a crack is longer than a dollar bill or sits in your line of sight, it needs attention.

How long does a windshield chip repair take?

A standard chip repair takes about 30 to 45 minutes once a technician is on site. The resin needs a few minutes to cure under UV light before you can drive, so plan for the visit to take under an hour from start to finish.

Is mobile auto glass repair as good as going into a shop?

Yes, for most repairs and replacements. The equipment and urethane adhesives used on a mobile visit are the same ones used in a shop bay. The only time a shop visit is preferred is when a vehicle needs a controlled indoor environment for calibration or has extensive rust around the frame that needs separate bodywork.

Do I need ADAS calibration after a windshield replacement?

If your vehicle has a forward-facing camera mounted near the rearview mirror, for features like lane departure warning or automatic emergency braking, it almost always needs to be recalibrated after the windshield is replaced. Skipping calibration can cause those safety systems to misread the road.

What is the difference between a repairable chip and one that needs replacement?

A chip smaller than a quarter, away from the edge of the glass and outside your direct line of sight, is usually repairable. Cracks longer than about six inches, damage that reaches the edge of the windshield, or multiple chips clustered together typically call for a full replacement instead.
